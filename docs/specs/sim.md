# Simulation specification

This file specifies the v0 simulation rules of SuperCom: tick model, economy, build mechanics, categories and tech tiers, orders, weapons and projectiles, damage and armour and shields, health and veterancy and death and wreckage, movement layers, intel, victory, and initial state. Each clause names its evaluator. A clause whose evaluator is a script is machine-verified: the script reads only `SUPERCOM_FA_DIR`, reads archive members in memory, prints counts, constants, and identifiers, prints `SKIP:` and succeeds when the install is absent, fails under `SUPERCOM_REQUIRE_GAME=1`, and exits non-zero when its clause is false. A clause whose evaluator is a pinned source is cited, not run; it is agent-checked. Counts below are those of the installed Forged Alliance data (568 unit blueprints in `units.scd`). Statements about the original engine rest on a pinned citation or a script-verified data fact; a choice of this project is a design clause and says so. The engine-API inventory at the end classifies every engine-exposed function and every Lua-side behaviour group.

## Tick model

**SIM-001** `reference` `source:https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FAForever/fa/041f661457b0/engine/User.lua#L572`
The nominal simulation rate of the original engine MUST be taken as 10 simulation ticks per second: FAF's engine stub documents GetSimTicksPerSecond as returning a hardcoded constant of how many sim ticks are supposed to occur per second and annotates its return as 10. The stub states no value for SecondsPerTick, GetGameTimeSeconds, or GetGameTick, so this clause is the only source of the rate.

**SIM-002** `design` `source:docs/adr/0003-render-and-sim-stack.md`
The sim's tick length constant MUST be 0.1 seconds of simulated time per tick, derived from SIM-001 as the reciprocal of 10 ticks per second, and simulated time MUST be held as an integer tick count, never as a wall-clock reading. This confirms the 10 Hz target that the stack decision left to the specification.

**SIM-003** `game-data` `script:tools/spec_eval/sim/tick.py SIM-003`
The installed lua.scd MUST reference the tick accessors in exactly this pattern: GetSimTicksPerSecond in 1 member (lua/ui/game/economy.lua), SecondsPerTick in 1 member (lua/sim/tasks/EnhanceTask.lua), GetGameTimeSeconds in 10 members, and GetGameTick in none; 12 members reference at least one of the first three.

**SIM-004** `engine-stub` `source:https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FAForever/fa/041f661457b0/engine/Sim.lua#L643`
The sim MUST expose the current tick as an integer count, as the stub declares GetGameTick at line 643 of engine/Sim.lua with an integer return, and MUST expose elapsed simulated time in seconds and the seconds-per-tick constant as numbers, as the stub declares GetGameTimeSeconds (engine/Core.lua line 237) and SecondsPerTick (engine/Core.lua line 418). The stubs fix shape only.

**SIM-005** `design` `source:docs/adr/0003-render-and-sim-stack.md`
The realised wall-clock duration of a tick at each game speed is unmeasured and no source states it, so the sim MUST NOT depend on it and the application layer MAY pace ticks by any policy; this is a low-priority clause whose signpost is a comparison of replay tick counts against wall-clock duration at each game speed. It never qualifies the nominal rate of SIM-001.

## Economy and build rules

**SIM-040** `design` `test:sim_stall_grants_fraction_and_clamps_storage`
In the economy phase each army's requests MUST be settled against what is available, stored plus income: the fraction of a resource granted is 1 when nothing of it is requested and otherwise the lesser of 1 and the available amount over the requested amount; an entity that requests several resources MUST be granted the lowest of its resources' fractions of each request, so a unit short of one resource consumes the other at the same fraction; the stored amount after the tick is the available amount less the amount granted, never above the army's storage and never below 0; and the stall counter MUST increase by one in every tick in which any army's fraction of any resource is below 1. The original's arithmetic is unreadable and its behaviour under a shortage is unobserved, so the rule is this project's choice, recorded in `docs/adr/0011-economy-and-build.md`. The test stalls two builders on 10 stored mass against 16 requested and an energy-only consumer beside them, and compares the fractions, the grants, the stores, the counter, and the next tick.

**SIM-041** `derived` `test:sim_extractor_requires_a_mass_marker`
Following from SIM-054 and SIM-049, a unit kind whose build restriction is OnMassDeposit MUST be placed only within 1 world unit, exclusive, on each of x, y, and z of a Mass marker that no other entity of such a kind holds, as the installed extractor code compares its position with a marker's, and the kind's mass production MUST be its blueprint value, the installed factor of marker amount over 100 being 1 on every installed marker (SIM-054), so the sim's marker carries no amount. The test refuses a site off every marker, on a Hydrocarbon marker, and exactly 1 unit from a Mass marker, accepts a site half a unit away, refuses a second extractor on a held marker, and compares the completed extractor's income and maintenance request in the ledger.

**SIM-042** `design` `test:sim_hydrocarbon_plant_on_marker`

**SIM-043** `design` `test:sim_producer_scales_by_maintenance_fraction_only`
A complete unit's production MUST be multiplied by the fraction granted to its maintenance request in the last economy phase, 1 when it makes none, and never by the fraction of a build request it makes, because the installed code that divides the mass production by the consumed fraction (SIM-053) shows the engine scaling a consuming producer while the ten commanders carry Economy.NaturalProducer (SIM-055), and a producer scaled by its own build request would freeze at empty stores; a mass fabricator MUST keep requesting its maintenance energy under a shortage, never pausing itself, because the auto-pause identifiers occur in no installed member (SIM-053). The economy phase MUST record each entity's maintenance fraction from its maintenance request's resources alone. The ruling is the head's, recorded in `docs/adr/0011-economy-and-build.md`. The test builds with a commander short of mass and compares its income with its full production, and starves a fabricator of energy and compares its income and fractions over three ticks.

**SIM-064** `design` `test:sim_layer_assignment_from_motion_and_terrain`

**SIM-065** `design` `test:sim_integration_accelerates_brakes_and_turns`
Each tick the movement phase MUST advance an entity with a route or a goal by the following binary64 operations, in this order, with every sine, cosine, arc tangent, and square root from the libm crate (DET-018 to DET-020): let (tx, tz) be the centre of the route's front cell, or the goal's x and z when the route is empty; dx = tx - x; dz = tz - z; desired = atan2(dz, dx); delta = desired - heading, wrapped into (-pi, pi] by subtracting 2 pi while delta exceeds pi and adding 2 pi while delta is at most -pi; when the kind has a turn rate, max_turn = turn_rate * (pi / 180) * dt, and the step is clamped when the absolute delta exceeds max_turn, in which case the unit is still turning and heading' = heading + delta clamped to [-max_turn, max_turn], wrapped the same way; otherwise, and always for a kind without a turn rate, which turns at once and is never still turning, heading' = desired exactly, with no rounding residue; remaining = the sum, front to back from 0, of the lengths sqrt(ddx * ddx + ddz * ddz) of the segments from (x, z) through the centres of the route's cells to the goal; let the brake rate be the kind's brake when it has one, else its acceleration when it has one, else none, the instant stop; when either the unit is still turning or remaining <= speed * speed / (2 * brake rate) (remaining <= 0 for the instant stop), speed' = max(0, speed - brake rate * dt) (speed' = 0 for the instant stop); otherwise speed' = min(max_speed, speed + acceleration * dt) when the kind has an acceleration, else speed' = max_speed; s = speed' * dt; when the route is empty and s is at least the distance sqrt(dx * dx + dz * dz), the entity is placed at the goal exactly, else x' = x + s * cos(heading') and z' = z + s * sin(heading'). An entity whose new cell is absent or impassable on its layer MUST keep its place and route, take speed 0 and heading', and never clip through a cliff. Route cells MUST then be consumed from the front while the front centre is within ARRIVAL_RADIUS, 0.5, of the entity, within meaning dx * dx + dz * dz <= ARRIVAL_RADIUS * ARRIVAL_RADIUS. No readable source states the engine's integration, steering, or pathing (SIM-062), so the rule is this project's choice, recorded in `docs/adr/0012-movement.md`, with deviations: the 47 air units without a turn rate or an acceleration and the 71 movers without a brake (SIM-063) take the instant defaults of the absent terms, and the Air block is not read; a kind without a brake uses its acceleration as the brake rate and a kind with neither stops at once whenever a braking rule applies, whatever its motion type, so no ground or naval mover circles a waypoint inside its turning circle: seven ground kinds carry no MaxBrake, the four commanders among them, and circled under the earlier default of never braking, and an air kind with neither field stops at once when it must brake, the original's air flight being unobserved. The turning test is exact: a residue near 5e-18 left by the heading sum on a start away from a cell centre, which the earlier test of a non-zero wrapped angle read as turning, braked 16 of one tank's 20 braking ticks; `crates/sim/tests/turning.rs` pins the speeds of an off-centre start against the clamped-step rule. The test compares position, speed, heading, and route bit for bit with its own restatement on every tick of a kind with every term, of a kind with none, and of a turn-limited kind, and pins the first three speeds of 0.1, then 0.1 and 0.1, which are not 0.3 in binary64, and brakes a fast turn-limited kind facing away from a goal one unit off until it faces the goal and arrives.

**SIM-066** `design` `test:sim_planner_shortest_path_and_no_corner_cutting`
The route planner MUST be an A* search over one layer's passability grid on the eight-neighbour lattice with integer costs of 10 for an orthogonal step and 14 for a diagonal one, a diagonal step allowed only when both orthogonal cells it cuts are passable, and the heuristic 10 times the larger of the axis distances plus 4 times the smaller, which is admissible and consistent for those costs; its result MUST be the cells from the first step to the goal cell, excluding the start, and MUST be absent when the start or the goal is impassable or off the grid or no route exists, and empty when they are one cell. Planning with A* and these costs is this project's choice (SIM-033), the original's planner being unreadable, recorded in `docs/adr/0012-movement.md`. The test plans around a wall with one gap and compares the route's cost with a relaxation search, refuses a diagonal with one or both orthogonals blocked, and returns nothing for an unreachable, impassable, or off-grid cell and for a layer without a grid.

**SIM-067** `design` `test:sim_planner_breaks_ties_by_cell_index`
The planner MUST be a pure function of its grid, layer, start, and goal, with ties broken by ascending cell index, the index being z times the width plus x (SIM-033): the open set is a binary heap ordered by the cost estimate ascending, then the cost so far descending, then the cell index ascending, so that among equal estimates the cell furthest along is expanded first; a cell's eight neighbours are examined in ascending index; a neighbour's recorded cost and parent are replaced only by a strictly smaller cost, so an equal cost keeps the earlier parent; and a popped cell that is already closed is skipped; the rule is recorded in `docs/adr/0012-movement.md`. The test generates 64 seeded 16 by 16 grids with two equal-cost corridors and 64 with one, plans each twice, and compares the route with the one the index rule selects by construction and with a control planner, written in the test, that prefers higher indices on ties: the control differs on every two-corridor grid and agrees on every one-corridor grid; a hand-built grid states the selected route; `crates/sim/tests/planner_expansions.rs` counts the cells a plan across an open 32 by 32 field from a corner to the far edge's middle expands against a planner written there under the same order.
